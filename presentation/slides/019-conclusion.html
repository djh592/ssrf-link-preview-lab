What Did We Learn?

Supported Answer

Within our tested conditions, Check & Bind was the only evaluated policy that met both security and preview goals.

Strongest Evidence

None of its 200 HTTP attack trials reached the internal report; the matched DNS probe stayed on the approved external peer; and all 150 normal previews succeeded.

For intelligent systems that act on untrusted input, validation must constrain the action that is actually executed.

Our security question asked whether a defense could prevent private access without breaking useful public previews. Within our tested conditions, the answer is yes: Check and Bind was the only evaluated policy that addressed both observed destination-change mechanisms while preserving every normal preview scenario. The strongest supporting result combines the security and utility evidence. Across the repeated HTTP experiments, none of the 200 Check and Bind attack trials reached the internal report. In the matched DNS mechanism probe, the transport remained bound to the approved external peer. All 150 normal previews succeeded. The HTTP figures are repeated trial results; the DNS statement describes one matched probe rather than an estimated attack rate. The broader lesson applies beyond link previews. When a system acts on untrusted input, validating the input is not enough unless that decision constrains the action the system ultimately executes.
