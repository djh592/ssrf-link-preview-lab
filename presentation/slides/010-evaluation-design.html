We measure both security and the usefulness of normal previews.

The Web UI showed individual examples of the attacks and defenses. Those runs explain the mechanisms, but they are not our measurements. For the evaluation, we compared five policies in controlled batch experiments. No Check performs no destination validation. Initial URL checks only the submitted URL. Each Hop checks every redirected URL. Check and Bind also binds the connection to the checked IP. No Redirects refuses to follow redirects. We ran every policy 50 times for each HTTP scenario. [Click] First, we tested normal previews. We used a direct page, a page with one redirect, and a page with three redirects. A trial succeeds when the expected preview content is returned. We also measure latency. Three scenarios, five policies, and 50 repetitions give 750 normal trials. [Click] Second, we tested S-S-R-F attacks. We used a private IP, a private service name, and attacks with one or three redirects. We measure whether the report receives the request and whether its protected marker is disclosed. Four scenarios, five policies, and 50 repetitions give 1,000 attack trials. Together, we ran 1,750 repeated HTTP trials. [Click] DNS rebinding is reported separately. We recorded four individual traces that connect the DNS answers, the actual HTTP peer, and the report events. These traces show the mechanism, but they do not estimate an attack rate. [Click] So our evaluation measures both sides of the research question: security against private access, and the usefulness of normal previews.
