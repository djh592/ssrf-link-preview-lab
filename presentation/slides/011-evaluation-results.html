Check & bind closed both observed gaps without sacrificing tested preview behavior.

Each HTTP attack row combines two scenarios, 50 trials per scenario and policy. The direct row combines a private IP and a private service name; the redirect row combines one and three redirects to the internal report. Values count requests reaching the report. Internal access and marker disclosure were measured separately and agreed in all 1,000 attack trials. For DNS rebinding, the table shows two individual probes under the same TTL-zero, empty-cache condition. Each hop connected to the internal report; Check and bind stayed on the approved external peer. The other policies were not tested in this condition. These probes illustrate the mechanism and do not estimate an attack rate. The normal row covers three scenarios, 50 trials each. The No redirects policy rejected all 100 legitimate redirect tasks. For direct pages, the median worker-side latency was 5.7 ms without a check and 6.9 ms with Check and bind; P95 was 57.6 and 62.5 ms. Each is based on 50 successful trials in the isolated lab, with image fetching disabled. Check and bind blocked the repeated HTTP attacks, kept the external peer in the matched DNS probe, and preserved all tested normal previews. These findings apply to the tested cases and environment.
