Limitations

Where the Evidence Stops

01Redirects

We tested direct requests and chains of 1 or 3 redirects in controlled HTTP fixtures.

02DNS rebinding

Four single probes cannot establish a success rate across TTL and cache conditions.

In the TTL 1, primed-cache probe, the each-hop policy stayed on the external address.
03Broader coverage

We evaluated one HTTP client stack and selected IPv4 scenarios; HTTPS and IPv6 were not measured.

Our Findings Apply to the Tested Scenarios and Environment.

Our results answer the research question within the tested environment, but they do not cover every possible link preview system. [Click] First, our repeated HTTP experiments cover direct requests and chains of one or three redirects. They do not tell us what happens with longer chains, different hosts, or protocol changes. [Click] Second, our DNS evidence contains only four individual probes, so we cannot report a general attack success rate. In the TTL-one probe with a primed cache, Each Hop stayed on the external address, and the attack did not reach the report. This single observation shows that cache and TTL conditions can change the outcome, but it is not a general conclusion. [Click] Third, we evaluated one Python and PycURL client stack using selected IPv4 and HTTP scenarios. We did not evaluate HTTPS, IPv6, other HTTP clients, or production network controls. [Click] Therefore, our findings apply to the scenarios and environment that we actually tested. We should not claim that they prove security for every implementation.
