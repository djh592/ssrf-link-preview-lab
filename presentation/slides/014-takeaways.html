Finally, let us return to our main research question. [Click] Within our tested conditions, the answer is yes. Check and Bind was the only evaluated policy that blocked both observed destination-change attacks while preserving all tested normal previews. [Click] The strongest evidence is straightforward. None of its 200 HTTP attack trials reached the internal report. The matched DNS probe stayed on the approved external peer, and all 150 normal previews succeeded. The HTTP numbers come from repeated trials, while the DNS result is one matched mechanism probe. [Click] The broader lesson applies beyond link previews. When an intelligent system acts on untrusted input, validating the input alone is not enough. Validation must constrain the action that is actually executed.
