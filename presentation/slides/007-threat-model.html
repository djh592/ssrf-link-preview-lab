Threat model

Our Threat Model

ATTACK An unauthorized request reaches the report
DISCLOSURE The report marker returns in the preview

The attacker cannot reach the report directly, but can influence a service that can.

Before presenting our defense, we need to define our threat model. The attacker is a normal user who is allowed to submit URLs. The preview service and the internal report are trusted, which means the attacker cannot modify them. [Click] The attacker has no direct route to the internal report. However, the preview service can reach it from inside the private network. [Click] The attacker can choose the submitted URL, control redirects and HTTP responses from their own server, and control DNS answers for their own domain. The attacker cannot change the preview service or the internal report. [Click] The attacker's goal is to use these inputs to make the preview service send an unauthorized request to the internal report. [Click] We measure two outcomes separately. We count an attack when the request reaches the report. We count disclosure only when the protected marker comes back in the preview. [Click] In short, the attacker cannot reach the report directly, but can influence a service that can.
